## Privacy gaps to close

The current Privacy Policy is a useful truthful summary, but it does not yet
contain every item in the ICO's right-to-be-informed checklist. A qualified
reviewer should approve an updated notice containing, where applicable:

1. the controller's full legal identity, registered/contact address and any
   representative or DPO details;
2. each processing purpose and lawful basis, including the legitimate interest
   relied on and the balancing assessment where that basis is used;
3. concrete retention periods or criteria for accounts, waitlist entries,
   onboarding, support mail, journals/reviews, security logs, billing metadata
   and backups;
4. recipients/categories, international transfers and safeguards;
5. all applicable rights, how to exercise them, the one-month response rule and
   the right to complain to the ICO;
6. whether data is required by contract/law and what happens if it is not
   provided;
7. any personal-data source not obtained directly from the person;
8. whether automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effect
   occur. The current decision engine is about markets, not automated decisions
   about a member, but the final notice should say so if confirmed;
9. age/children position and change-notification process.

The ICO says organisations must identify an appropriate lawful basis, be open
about their processing and not keep personal data longer than needed. It also
recommends documented standard retention periods and periodic deletion or
anonymisation.

## Cookie and device-storage gate

`COOKIE_AND_DEVICE_STORAGE_INVENTORY.md` now records every first-party cookie,
local/session-storage key and PWA cache found in source. The repository contains
no marketing analytics tracker. Keep that position for the cash-constrained
launch. Before public release:

- inventory every cookie and storage key, its purpose, owner and lifetime;
- confirm which items are strictly necessary;
- ensure optional third-party content is not loaded until the member acts;
- add a consent mechanism before any future non-essential cookie, pixel or
  similar device access is introduced;
- keep the customer-facing explanation aligned with the verified inventory.

ICO guidance states that cookies and similar device-storage technologies need
clear information and consent unless an exemption such as strict necessity
applies. Simply continuing to use a website is not sufficient consent for
non-essential storage.

## FCA perimeter and promotion gate

Bullseye's disclaimers and educational positioning reduce risk but do not, by
themselves, decide the UK regulatory perimeter. The FCA describes a financial
promotion broadly as an invitation or inducement to engage in investment
activity communicated in business, including websites, emails, adverts and
social posts. Social promotions must be fair, clear and not misleading; complex
products and limited-space formats require particular care.

Before any public advertising or paid launch, a qualified UK financial-services
lawyer/compliance professional must answer in writing:

1. whether the exact subscription product, personalised level tools, decision
   language, alerts, examples, videos and social posts are regulated advice,
   guidance or financial promotions;
2. whether Bullseye or its operator needs FCA authorisation/registration or can
   rely on a defined exclusion/exemption;
3. whether any communication requires approval under section 21 of FSMA and,
   if so, which FCA-authorised firm has the necessary approver permission;
4. which UK and non-UK jurisdictions may be targeted or must be excluded;
5. the exact prominent risk warnings and evidence needed for each claim;
6. whether checkout, cancellation/refund, auto-renewal, Founding price-lock and
   digital-service terms meet UK consumer law.

Until those answers are signed, keep all public/paid promotion factual,
waitlist-only and free of performance, profit, urgency or actionable-trade
claims. Do not represent Bullseye as FCA authorised.

## Lowest-cost approval route

1. Complete the free ICO privacy-notice checklist, lawful-basis exercise,
   storage inventory and fee self-assessment.
2. Ask the ICO small-business advice service about the specific notice,
   retention and fee questions.
3. Use the free Business Gateway/Find Business Support route for a business
   adviser and ask whether funded specialist legal help is available locally.
4. Apply to FCA Innovation Pathways with the developed Bullseye proposition and
   the six perimeter questions above. The FCA says it supports regulated and
   non-regulated firms of any size, including start-ups, but expressly says the
   service is not a replacement for legal/consultancy support.
5. Buy only one tightly scoped professional review: exact website, Terms,
   Privacy Policy, launch video, initial social/advert copy, target jurisdiction
   and perimeter/section-21 conclusion. Request a fixed fee before authorising
   work.
